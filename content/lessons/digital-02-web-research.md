# Navigating & Verifying the Web

> Navigate websites efficiently, judge sources by evidence rather than domain alone, cite information, and verify online or AI-generated claims before sharing them.

## Learning objectives

- Explain Source credibility in your own words.
- Apply Citation to a realistic classroom or community example.
- Complete the practice task and reflect on one improvement.

## Lesson notes

### 1. Source credibility

Trust earned through relevant expertise, transparent evidence, current information, accountable authorship, and confirmation from independent sources.

### 2. Citation

A structured reference that helps readers identify and return to the source of an idea, fact, image, or quotation.

### 3. Lateral reading

Opening other sources to investigate who is behind a claim, what evidence supports it, and what reliable organizations say about it.

## Guided practice

Research one community question using two independent sources. Record author, date, evidence, possible bias, and a citation, then explain why the sources agree or differ.

Work in pairs when devices are shared. Write your prediction before using a device, then compare the result with what actually happened.

## Check your understanding

- What is the most important idea from this lesson?
- Where could you use it at school, at work, or in your community?
- What would you teach to someone seeing this topic for the first time?
