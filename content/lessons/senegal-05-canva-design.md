## Why this lesson matters

Create clear visual communication in Canva by starting from purpose, choosing a strong template, and keeping typography, color, imagery, and branding consistent.

The goal is not to memorize vocabulary. By the end of the lesson, you should be able to use the ideas in a realistic situation, explain the reason for your choices, and check whether the result actually works for the intended person or task.

## Learning objectives

- Explain Design purpose in your own words.
- Apply Brand consistency to a realistic classroom or community example.
- Connect Design purpose with Template when making a decision.
- Complete the practice task and reflect on one improvement.

## Core ideas

### 1. Design purpose

The audience, message, and action a visual must support; every design decision should make that purpose easier to understand.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Design purpose changes what you choose, create, or check.

### 2. Brand consistency

Repeated use of a defined logo, color palette, typography, voice, and spacing so materials feel connected and recognizable.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Brand consistency changes what you choose, create, or check.

### 3. Template

A reusable starting layout that speeds up production but still needs thoughtful editing to fit the actual audience and message.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Template changes what you choose, create, or check.

## How the ideas connect

Start with **Design purpose** to understand the foundation of the lesson. Use **Brand consistency** to turn that understanding into an action. Then apply **Template** to check the quality, safety, or usefulness of the result. The three ideas are strongest when you can explain their relationship rather than treating them as separate definitions.

## Guided walkthrough

1. **Name the goal.** In one sentence, write what you are trying to understand, create, or improve.
2. **Make a prediction.** Before touching a device, use Design purpose and Brand consistency to predict what a strong result should look like.
3. **Complete the task.** Create a one-page community event poster with one headline, three essential details, one call to action, and a limited palette. Test if a partner understands it in five seconds.
4. **Check the outcome.** Use Template to inspect the result. Ask what worked, what did not, and what evidence supports your judgment.
5. **Explain and revise.** Tell a partner what you changed and why. Make one small improvement, then compare the new result with the first one.

## Worked classroom scenario

Imagine two learners sharing one device. The first learner is the **driver** and performs the steps; the second is the **navigator** and reads the goal, predicts the next step, and checks the result. Halfway through the task, switch roles. Both learners should be able to explain how Design purpose, Brand consistency, and Template appeared in the work.

If no device is available, complete the same reasoning on paper: sketch the screen or result, label each decision, and describe what you would test when a device becomes available.

## Common mistakes and fixes

- **Rushing into the tool:** Write the goal and prediction first so every click or step has a reason.
- **Copying without understanding:** After each major step, explain it in your own words to a partner.
- **Accepting the first result:** Compare the outcome with the goal and make at least one deliberate improvement.
- **Letting one person control a shared device:** Rotate driver and navigator roles so both learners think and practice.

## Independent practice

Create a one-page community event poster with one headline, three essential details, one call to action, and a limited palette. Test if a partner understands it in five seconds.

For an extra challenge, adapt the task for a different audience or community need. Write two sentences explaining what changed and which lesson idea guided your decision.

## Check your understanding

1. How would you explain Design purpose to someone new to the topic?
2. What is one realistic example of Brand consistency outside this classroom?
3. When might Template prevent a weak, unsafe, or confusing result?
4. How are Design purpose and Brand consistency connected?
5. What evidence would convince you that your practice result works?
6. If you repeated the activity tomorrow, what would you improve first and why?

## Key takeaway

Create clear visual communication in Canva by starting from purpose, choosing a strong template, and keeping typography, color, imagery, and branding consistent.

You are ready to move on when you can explain the three core ideas, complete the practice without copying, and describe one improvement using evidence from your result.
