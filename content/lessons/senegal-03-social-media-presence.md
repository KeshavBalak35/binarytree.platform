## Why this lesson matters

Use free social platforms to communicate across distance, share work consistently, build trust, and discover opportunities while keeping a professional identity.

The goal is not to memorize vocabulary. By the end of the lesson, you should be able to use the ideas in a realistic situation, explain the reason for your choices, and check whether the result actually works for the intended person or task.

## Learning objectives

- Explain Digital presence in your own words.
- Apply Consistent publishing to a realistic classroom or community example.
- Connect Digital presence with Engagement when making a decision.
- Complete the practice task and reflect on one improvement.

## Core ideas

### 1. Digital presence

The combined impression created by your profiles, posts, portfolio, and interactions across online platforms.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Digital presence changes what you choose, create, or check.

### 2. Consistent publishing

Sharing useful, relevant work on a sustainable schedule so an audience can understand your focus and trust your commitment.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Consistent publishing changes what you choose, create, or check.

### 3. Engagement

Meaningful audience actions such as saves, comments, replies, shares, or conversations—not only the number of followers.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Engagement changes what you choose, create, or check.

## How the ideas connect

Start with **Digital presence** to understand the foundation of the lesson. Use **Consistent publishing** to turn that understanding into an action. Then apply **Engagement** to check the quality, safety, or usefulness of the result. The three ideas are strongest when you can explain their relationship rather than treating them as separate definitions.

## Guided walkthrough

1. **Name the goal.** In one sentence, write what you are trying to understand, create, or improve.
2. **Make a prediction.** Before touching a device, use Digital presence and Consistent publishing to predict what a strong result should look like.
3. **Complete the task.** Draft a professional profile: one-sentence purpose, three skills, one proof-of-work post, and a safe call to action. Review it with a partner before publishing anything.
4. **Check the outcome.** Use Engagement to inspect the result. Ask what worked, what did not, and what evidence supports your judgment.
5. **Explain and revise.** Tell a partner what you changed and why. Make one small improvement, then compare the new result with the first one.

## Worked classroom scenario

Imagine two learners sharing one device. The first learner is the **driver** and performs the steps; the second is the **navigator** and reads the goal, predicts the next step, and checks the result. Halfway through the task, switch roles. Both learners should be able to explain how Digital presence, Consistent publishing, and Engagement appeared in the work.

If no device is available, complete the same reasoning on paper: sketch the screen or result, label each decision, and describe what you would test when a device becomes available.

## Common mistakes and fixes

- **Rushing into the tool:** Write the goal and prediction first so every click or step has a reason.
- **Copying without understanding:** After each major step, explain it in your own words to a partner.
- **Accepting the first result:** Compare the outcome with the goal and make at least one deliberate improvement.
- **Letting one person control a shared device:** Rotate driver and navigator roles so both learners think and practice.

## Independent practice

Draft a professional profile: one-sentence purpose, three skills, one proof-of-work post, and a safe call to action. Review it with a partner before publishing anything.

For an extra challenge, adapt the task for a different audience or community need. Write two sentences explaining what changed and which lesson idea guided your decision.

## Check your understanding

1. How would you explain Digital presence to someone new to the topic?
2. What is one realistic example of Consistent publishing outside this classroom?
3. When might Engagement prevent a weak, unsafe, or confusing result?
4. How are Digital presence and Consistent publishing connected?
5. What evidence would convince you that your practice result works?
6. If you repeated the activity tomorrow, what would you improve first and why?

## Key takeaway

Use free social platforms to communicate across distance, share work consistently, build trust, and discover opportunities while keeping a professional identity.

You are ready to move on when you can explain the three core ideas, complete the practice without copying, and describe one improvement using evidence from your result.
