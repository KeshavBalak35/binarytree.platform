## Why this lesson matters

Use social platforms intentionally: protect your account, shape a credible professional presence, and build relationships without sharing information that creates risk.

The goal is not to memorize vocabulary. By the end of the lesson, you should be able to use the ideas in a realistic situation, explain the reason for your choices, and check whether the result actually works for the intended person or task.

## Learning objectives

- Explain Digital footprint in your own words.
- Apply Professional network to a realistic classroom or community example.
- Connect Digital footprint with Account safety when making a decision.
- Complete the practice task and reflect on one improvement.

## Core ideas

### 1. Digital footprint

The lasting record created by posts, comments, likes, profiles, and other online activity that can shape how others see you.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Digital footprint changes what you choose, create, or check.

### 2. Professional network

A group of people and organizations connected through shared work, learning, or goals who can exchange knowledge and opportunities.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Professional network changes what you choose, create, or check.

### 3. Account safety

Using unique passwords, multi-factor authentication, privacy controls, and careful link checking to reduce the chance of account loss or fraud.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Account safety changes what you choose, create, or check.

## How the ideas connect

Start with **Digital footprint** to understand the foundation of the lesson. Use **Professional network** to turn that understanding into an action. Then apply **Account safety** to check the quality, safety, or usefulness of the result. The three ideas are strongest when you can explain their relationship rather than treating them as separate definitions.

## Guided walkthrough

1. **Name the goal.** In one sentence, write what you are trying to understand, create, or improve.
2. **Make a prediction.** Before touching a device, use Digital footprint and Professional network to predict what a strong result should look like.
3. **Complete the task.** Audit a sample social profile. Mark what builds trust, what should stay private, and one change that would make the profile more useful for networking.
4. **Check the outcome.** Use Account safety to inspect the result. Ask what worked, what did not, and what evidence supports your judgment.
5. **Explain and revise.** Tell a partner what you changed and why. Make one small improvement, then compare the new result with the first one.

## Worked classroom scenario

Imagine two learners sharing one device. The first learner is the **driver** and performs the steps; the second is the **navigator** and reads the goal, predicts the next step, and checks the result. Halfway through the task, switch roles. Both learners should be able to explain how Digital footprint, Professional network, and Account safety appeared in the work.

If no device is available, complete the same reasoning on paper: sketch the screen or result, label each decision, and describe what you would test when a device becomes available.

## Common mistakes and fixes

- **Rushing into the tool:** Write the goal and prediction first so every click or step has a reason.
- **Copying without understanding:** After each major step, explain it in your own words to a partner.
- **Accepting the first result:** Compare the outcome with the goal and make at least one deliberate improvement.
- **Letting one person control a shared device:** Rotate driver and navigator roles so both learners think and practice.

## Independent practice

Audit a sample social profile. Mark what builds trust, what should stay private, and one change that would make the profile more useful for networking.

For an extra challenge, adapt the task for a different audience or community need. Write two sentences explaining what changed and which lesson idea guided your decision.

## Check your understanding

1. How would you explain Digital footprint to someone new to the topic?
2. What is one realistic example of Professional network outside this classroom?
3. When might Account safety prevent a weak, unsafe, or confusing result?
4. How are Digital footprint and Professional network connected?
5. What evidence would convince you that your practice result works?
6. If you repeated the activity tomorrow, what would you improve first and why?

## Key takeaway

Use social platforms intentionally: protect your account, shape a credible professional presence, and build relationships without sharing information that creates risk.

You are ready to move on when you can explain the three core ideas, complete the practice without copying, and describe one improvement using evidence from your result.
