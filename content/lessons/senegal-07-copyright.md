## Why this lesson matters

Recognize creative work protected by copyright, document your own creation process, seek permission before reuse, and understand why business registration differs by country.

The goal is not to memorize vocabulary. By the end of the lesson, you should be able to use the ideas in a realistic situation, explain the reason for your choices, and check whether the result actually works for the intended person or task.

## Learning objectives

- Explain Copyright in your own words.
- Apply License to a realistic classroom or community example.
- Connect Copyright with Attribution when making a decision.
- Complete the practice task and reflect on one improvement.

## Core ideas

### 1. Copyright

Legal protection that gives creators control over copying, distributing, adapting, displaying, or monetizing an original work.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Copyright changes what you choose, create, or check.

### 2. License

Permission that states how another person may use a work, including conditions such as payment, attribution, or limits on modification.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how License changes what you choose, create, or check.

### 3. Attribution

A clear credit naming the creator and source; it is required by some licenses but does not replace permission when permission is needed.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Attribution changes what you choose, create, or check.

## How the ideas connect

Start with **Copyright** to understand the foundation of the lesson. Use **License** to turn that understanding into an action. Then apply **Attribution** to check the quality, safety, or usefulness of the result. The three ideas are strongest when you can explain their relationship rather than treating them as separate definitions.

## Guided walkthrough

1. **Name the goal.** In one sentence, write what you are trying to understand, create, or improve.
2. **Make a prediction.** Before touching a device, use Copyright and License to predict what a strong result should look like.
3. **Complete the task.** Review four sample assets for a business poster. Decide which you created, licensed, may use with attribution, or should replace. Record the source for every accepted asset.
4. **Check the outcome.** Use Attribution to inspect the result. Ask what worked, what did not, and what evidence supports your judgment.
5. **Explain and revise.** Tell a partner what you changed and why. Make one small improvement, then compare the new result with the first one.

## Worked classroom scenario

Imagine two learners sharing one device. The first learner is the **driver** and performs the steps; the second is the **navigator** and reads the goal, predicts the next step, and checks the result. Halfway through the task, switch roles. Both learners should be able to explain how Copyright, License, and Attribution appeared in the work.

If no device is available, complete the same reasoning on paper: sketch the screen or result, label each decision, and describe what you would test when a device becomes available.

## Common mistakes and fixes

- **Rushing into the tool:** Write the goal and prediction first so every click or step has a reason.
- **Copying without understanding:** After each major step, explain it in your own words to a partner.
- **Accepting the first result:** Compare the outcome with the goal and make at least one deliberate improvement.
- **Letting one person control a shared device:** Rotate driver and navigator roles so both learners think and practice.

## Independent practice

Review four sample assets for a business poster. Decide which you created, licensed, may use with attribution, or should replace. Record the source for every accepted asset.

For an extra challenge, adapt the task for a different audience or community need. Write two sentences explaining what changed and which lesson idea guided your decision.

## Check your understanding

1. How would you explain Copyright to someone new to the topic?
2. What is one realistic example of License outside this classroom?
3. When might Attribution prevent a weak, unsafe, or confusing result?
4. How are Copyright and License connected?
5. What evidence would convince you that your practice result works?
6. If you repeated the activity tomorrow, what would you improve first and why?

## Key takeaway

Recognize creative work protected by copyright, document your own creation process, seek permission before reuse, and understand why business registration differs by country.

You are ready to move on when you can explain the three core ideas, complete the practice without copying, and describe one improvement using evidence from your result.
