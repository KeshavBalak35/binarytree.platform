## Why this lesson matters

Build a clear, achievement-focused resume and write professional emails that make a strong first impression for jobs, partnerships, and entrepreneurship.

The goal is not to memorize vocabulary. By the end of the lesson, you should be able to use the ideas in a realistic situation, explain the reason for your choices, and check whether the result actually works for the intended person or task.

## Learning objectives

- Explain Targeted resume in your own words.
- Apply Quantified achievement to a realistic classroom or community example.
- Connect Targeted resume with Professional email when making a decision.
- Complete the practice task and reflect on one improvement.

## Core ideas

### 1. Targeted resume

A resume adjusted to match the skills and keywords in one specific opportunity instead of sending the same document everywhere.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Targeted resume changes what you choose, create, or check.

### 2. Quantified achievement

A result described with numbers, time, scale, or impact so a reader can understand what changed because of your work.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Quantified achievement changes what you choose, create, or check.

### 3. Professional email

A concise message with a useful subject, respectful greeting, clear purpose, requested next step, and complete signature.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Professional email changes what you choose, create, or check.

## How the ideas connect

Start with **Targeted resume** to understand the foundation of the lesson. Use **Quantified achievement** to turn that understanding into an action. Then apply **Professional email** to check the quality, safety, or usefulness of the result. The three ideas are strongest when you can explain their relationship rather than treating them as separate definitions.

## Guided walkthrough

1. **Name the goal.** In one sentence, write what you are trying to understand, create, or improve.
2. **Make a prediction.** Before touching a device, use Targeted resume and Quantified achievement to predict what a strong result should look like.
3. **Complete the task.** Choose a real opportunity. Rewrite one resume bullet with a measurable result, then draft a five-sentence email that introduces yourself and asks for a next step.
4. **Check the outcome.** Use Professional email to inspect the result. Ask what worked, what did not, and what evidence supports your judgment.
5. **Explain and revise.** Tell a partner what you changed and why. Make one small improvement, then compare the new result with the first one.

## Worked classroom scenario

Imagine two learners sharing one device. The first learner is the **driver** and performs the steps; the second is the **navigator** and reads the goal, predicts the next step, and checks the result. Halfway through the task, switch roles. Both learners should be able to explain how Targeted resume, Quantified achievement, and Professional email appeared in the work.

If no device is available, complete the same reasoning on paper: sketch the screen or result, label each decision, and describe what you would test when a device becomes available.

## Common mistakes and fixes

- **Rushing into the tool:** Write the goal and prediction first so every click or step has a reason.
- **Copying without understanding:** After each major step, explain it in your own words to a partner.
- **Accepting the first result:** Compare the outcome with the goal and make at least one deliberate improvement.
- **Letting one person control a shared device:** Rotate driver and navigator roles so both learners think and practice.

## Independent practice

Choose a real opportunity. Rewrite one resume bullet with a measurable result, then draft a five-sentence email that introduces yourself and asks for a next step.

For an extra challenge, adapt the task for a different audience or community need. Write two sentences explaining what changed and which lesson idea guided your decision.

## Check your understanding

1. How would you explain Targeted resume to someone new to the topic?
2. What is one realistic example of Quantified achievement outside this classroom?
3. When might Professional email prevent a weak, unsafe, or confusing result?
4. How are Targeted resume and Quantified achievement connected?
5. What evidence would convince you that your practice result works?
6. If you repeated the activity tomorrow, what would you improve first and why?

## Key takeaway

Build a clear, achievement-focused resume and write professional emails that make a strong first impression for jobs, partnerships, and entrepreneurship.

You are ready to move on when you can explain the three core ideas, complete the practice without copying, and describe one improvement using evidence from your result.
