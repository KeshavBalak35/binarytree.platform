## Why this lesson matters

Organize cloud files, communicate through Gmail, and collaborate safely in Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Drive using the right sharing permission.

The goal is not to memorize vocabulary. By the end of the lesson, you should be able to use the ideas in a realistic situation, explain the reason for your choices, and check whether the result actually works for the intended person or task.

## Learning objectives

- Explain Cloud workspace in your own words.
- Apply File organization to a realistic classroom or community example.
- Connect Cloud workspace with Sharing permission when making a decision.
- Complete the practice task and reflect on one improvement.

## Core ideas

### 1. Cloud workspace

A set of online tools that saves work centrally so it can be opened, edited, and synchronized across devices.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Cloud workspace changes what you choose, create, or check.

### 2. File organization

A consistent system of descriptive names, folders, stars, and versions that makes information quick to find and hard to lose.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how File organization changes what you choose, create, or check.

### 3. Sharing permission

The access level—viewer, commenter, or editor—that controls what another person can do with a file.

**In practice:** Look for this idea while you complete the lesson task. Pause before each major step and explain how Sharing permission changes what you choose, create, or check.

## How the ideas connect

Start with **Cloud workspace** to understand the foundation of the lesson. Use **File organization** to turn that understanding into an action. Then apply **Sharing permission** to check the quality, safety, or usefulness of the result. The three ideas are strongest when you can explain their relationship rather than treating them as separate definitions.

## Guided walkthrough

1. **Name the goal.** In one sentence, write what you are trying to understand, create, or improve.
2. **Make a prediction.** Before touching a device, use Cloud workspace and File organization to predict what a strong result should look like.
3. **Complete the task.** Create a project folder with three named subfolders, add one document to each, and explain when a collaborator should be an editor, commenter, or viewer.
4. **Check the outcome.** Use Sharing permission to inspect the result. Ask what worked, what did not, and what evidence supports your judgment.
5. **Explain and revise.** Tell a partner what you changed and why. Make one small improvement, then compare the new result with the first one.

## Worked classroom scenario

Imagine two learners sharing one device. The first learner is the **driver** and performs the steps; the second is the **navigator** and reads the goal, predicts the next step, and checks the result. Halfway through the task, switch roles. Both learners should be able to explain how Cloud workspace, File organization, and Sharing permission appeared in the work.

If no device is available, complete the same reasoning on paper: sketch the screen or result, label each decision, and describe what you would test when a device becomes available.

## Common mistakes and fixes

- **Rushing into the tool:** Write the goal and prediction first so every click or step has a reason.
- **Copying without understanding:** After each major step, explain it in your own words to a partner.
- **Accepting the first result:** Compare the outcome with the goal and make at least one deliberate improvement.
- **Letting one person control a shared device:** Rotate driver and navigator roles so both learners think and practice.

## Independent practice

Create a project folder with three named subfolders, add one document to each, and explain when a collaborator should be an editor, commenter, or viewer.

For an extra challenge, adapt the task for a different audience or community need. Write two sentences explaining what changed and which lesson idea guided your decision.

## Check your understanding

1. How would you explain Cloud workspace to someone new to the topic?
2. What is one realistic example of File organization outside this classroom?
3. When might Sharing permission prevent a weak, unsafe, or confusing result?
4. How are Cloud workspace and File organization connected?
5. What evidence would convince you that your practice result works?
6. If you repeated the activity tomorrow, what would you improve first and why?

## Key takeaway

Organize cloud files, communicate through Gmail, and collaborate safely in Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Drive using the right sharing permission.

You are ready to move on when you can explain the three core ideas, complete the practice without copying, and describe one improvement using evidence from your result.
